Who you are calling

How Bend Apex Plumbing works

Bend Apex Plumbing is a website and phone line that helps Central Oregon homeowners reach a plumber when something is wrong. It is an advertising and referral service, not a plumbing company with its own trucks. When you call, dispatch asks what is happening, talks you through the first safety steps, and connects you with a participating independent plumbing contractor.

We say this plainly because the licenses, insurance and workmanship belong to the contractor who comes to your door. Knowing that arrangement before you call lets you ask the right questions and verify the contractor yourself.

Before work starts

Check any plumber in four steps

What to verify

  • 1Ask for the contractor's business name and Construction Contractors Board license number, then search it on the state CCB lookup.
  • 2Check that the business also holds an Oregon plumbing contractor license from the Building Codes Division.
  • 3Ask for the name of the person doing the work and confirm that individual holds a plumbing license too.
  • 4Get the price in writing, or by text, before any emergency repair begins.
Standards

The code behind the work

Plumbing in Oregon is governed by the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which builds on the Uniform Plumbing Code and which the Building Codes Division adopts and enforces. It sets rules for venting, water heater installation, backflow protection and other details that affect safety. Repairs that replace rather than patch a system can require a permit and an inspection.

The pages on this site describe how problems are normally diagnosed and repaired under that framework. They are general information, not a diagnosis of your home, and they do not replace an inspection by a licensed plumber.

Limits

What this service does not do

We do not pump or service septic tanks, which is a separate licensed trade in Oregon. We do not give medical or health advice about drinking water, and any treatment decision should follow a lab test. We also do not promise arrival times, because weather and call volume change the estimate hour by hour.

Some problems that look like plumbing belong to another trade. Groundwater in a basement, a roof leak that stains a ceiling, or a mold concern after a leak are examples. The service pages say when to involve a roofer, a drying specialist or a testing lab so you do not pay a plumber to solve the wrong problem.

On the phone

What to expect when you call

Dispatch starts with safety. If water is moving, you will be asked to find the main shutoff. In many Bend homes it sits in a garage, a utility closet or a crawlspace hatch. If there is a gas smell, you will be told to leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility before anything else.

Next comes the address, including the ZIP code and the nearest cross street, then a short description of what you see, hear and smell. That is enough for dispatch to decide what to send. The job might need a well pump specialist, a drain machine or a water heater on the truck.

Before emergency work begins, the contractor explains what is wrong and gives you a price. You can say no, call someone else, and still keep the safety steps you were given. A price you agreed to is a price you can compare.

Who this is for

Homeowners, landlords and small businesses

Most calls come from owners of single-family homes. The line also serves landlords who need a drain cleared between tenants, and managers of small commercial spaces in Bend, Redmond and Sisters. A rental with a failed water heater and a tenant waiting on a shower is handled the same way as a home.

If you manage several properties, keep a note of each main shutoff location and each water source. Know in advance whether a building is on city water, Avion service or a private well. It saves several minutes on the first call, and it often decides which contractor is the right fit.

How the guides are written

Sources behind the local information

Local facts on this site come from public sources rather than guesses. Water hardness figures come from the City of Bend. Private well and nitrate information comes from the Oregon Department of Environmental Quality, the U.S. Geological Survey and Deschutes County. Licensing details come from the Oregon Building Codes Division and Construction Contractors Board.

Where a fact varies by street, such as the water source or the age of the plumbing, the page says so instead of making a blanket claim. If you spot something that looks wrong or out of date, email contact@apexplumberbend.com and the page will be reviewed.
