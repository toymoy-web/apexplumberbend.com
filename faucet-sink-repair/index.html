A repair kit often costs less than a hardware store lunch. The skill is identifying the faucet type, because cartridges are not interchangeable.

Under the sink

Supply stops, traps and drains cause half the leaks

Many sink leaks are not the faucet at all. A supply stop valve that weeps at the stem, a braided supply line with a worn connector, or a drain trap with a loose nut will drip into the cabinet for weeks. The tell is a damp or swollen cabinet floor.

A plumber checks the pieces from top to bottom: faucet body, supply lines, stops, drain assembly and trap, all of which fall under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Oregon. Replacing a corroded stop valve is a common part of a faucet job, since a stop that cannot close makes every later repair harder.

If the sink is slow, that points to the trap or a branch line, and drain cleaning and unclogging is the right page.

Repair or replace

Deciding between a new cartridge and a new faucet

When repair wins

A faucet under about ten years old from a known maker usually has parts available. A new cartridge or seal costs a fraction of replacement and restores like-new performance. Finish quality also matters, since pitting and flaking mean the faucet is aging out regardless of the drip.

Ask the plumber to name the faucet make and the part. A vague answer suggests guesswork.

When to replace

Replacement makes sense when parts are discontinued, the finish is failing, the body is corroded or you want a different style. Pull-down and touchless kitchen faucets add features that repair cannot.

Water usage matters too. Faucets generally flow no more than 2.2 gallons per minute under federal rules, and WaterSense models use less.

Before the plumber arrives

What to do about a leaking sink

Quick steps to limit damage

  • 1Close the supply stops under the sink by turning them clockwise.
  • 2If a stop will not close, shut the main water valve instead.
  • 3Empty the cabinet and dry the floor, then put a pan under the leak.
  • 4Take a photo of the damage and the faucet make for the plumber.
  • 5Do not tighten fittings with a wrench beyond snug, since you can crack plastic parts.
Water source matters

Why well homes clog aerators and cartridges sooner

City of Bend water is reported as soft to slightly hard, so scale is rarely the cause of a stuck cartridge in town. Homes on private wells can differ. Sand, rust flakes and mineral deposits from a well or an aging pressure tank collect in aerators and cartridges and show up as sputtering flow or a stiff handle.

If every faucet in a well home clogs with grit, the faucet is not the problem. A failing well screen, a worn pressure tank or a missing sediment filter is. Cleaning aerators fixes the symptom for a week, and the pump system needs a look to fix the cause, as described on the well pump service page.
