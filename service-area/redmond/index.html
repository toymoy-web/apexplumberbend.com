Housing era

Redmond's boom-era homes are now old enough to need plumbing work

Few Oregon cities expanded as quickly as Redmond did after 2000. Population figures show growth of roughly 74 percent from 2000 to 2006, so many of the subdivisions you drive past were framed in that stretch. Two decades on, appliances and valves installed during the rush are wearing out at nearly the same moment.

A plumber working Redmond nights sees the pattern repeatedly. A tank put in during 2005 is well beyond the midpoint of its life, and the fill valves, braided hoses and disposals beside it tend to quit within a year or two of each other. One bad weekend can send several calls from a single cul-de-sac.

Redmond sits at about 3,000 feet, roughly a thousand feet lower than Sunriver and La Pine, so freezes arrive but linger less than they do there. Cold snaps still catch exposed lines in garages and crawlspaces.

City services

Water, sewer and storm drains in Redmond

City water and sewer

The city's water comes from groundwater pumped from deep wells, drawn from a volcanic formation fed by Cascade snowmelt. Redmond runs its own wastewater plant, so homes inside the city are on public sewer, and a backup in the line past your cleanout may be the city's to clear.

If several neighbors have drains backing up at once, call the city as well as a plumber, since the cause may sit in the public main.

Drywells and heavy rain

Redmond handles stormwater with thousands of drywells, which suit the area's porous volcanic ground. A basement or crawlspace that floods after a storm is usually a grading or drainage matter, not a sewer problem.

A sump pump is a plumbing fixture, and one that has quit during a spring thaw is worth treating as urgent. A plumber can check the pump, the float and where the discharge goes.

Check these before the heater fails

Before you wait for a leak to tell you

  • 1Find the data label on the tank and note the manufacture date.
  • 2Look at the base and fittings for rust streaks or white mineral crust.
  • 3Listen for popping or rumbling, which signals sediment on the bottom.
  • 4Check that the relief valve discharge pipe is in place and not dripping.
  • 5If the tank is more than a dozen years old, ask for a replacement quote while it still works.

Before we arrive

Before we arrive in Redmond: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak

Most Redmond leaks start at a water heater, a supply line or a fill valve, so these steps begin there.

1

Shut off the main valve

In many Redmond homes built after 2000 the main shutoff sits in the garage near the water heater, or on the wall where the line enters the house. Turn the handle until it stops, then open the lowest faucet to drain the pipes. A lever closes with a quarter turn, and a round wheel takes several full turns.

2

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near the garage outlets, the laundry circuit or the water heater, switch those breakers off at the panel before you step into the room. Never touch a cord or a switch while standing in water. An electric tank should lose power before it drains, or the elements can burn out.

3

Clear the immediate area

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone, and set furniture legs on foil or blocks. Photograph the leak and the damage before you move anything, because your insurer will ask. If the leak is at the water heater, close its cold supply valve.

4

Call our 24/7 line

Call (503) 974-3329 and say where the water is coming from and whether the main is off. Mention if the heater is in a garage or a closet and whether you have a crawlspace. A short description helps dispatch send the right truck first.
