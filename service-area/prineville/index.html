City utilities

A county seat with its own city water and sewer department

Prineville is the county seat of Crook County, with 10,736 people counted in the 2020 census, and it sits at about 2,868 feet, roughly 770 feet lower than Bend. Highway 26 and Highway 126 bring most traffic in. A plumber working Prineville has two kinds of calls, homes inside the city and rural properties out in the county.

The city obtains its water from several wells spread over the system and stores it in ground-level reservoirs. Prineville's Water and Wastewater Department maintains the water and sewer lines inside the city limits. The city bought the system from Pacific Power and Light in January 1985, and some of the original piping has been replaced over the decades.

That history matters to a homeowner. A leak at the meter or a sewer main problem may belong to the city, while a leak inside the property line belongs to the owner. Ask the city where the public line ends and yours begins before you authorize digging.

Which side of the meter

Who fixes what in a Prineville leak

If water is coming up in the street or the yard near the meter, call the city's water department for the public side. The after-hours number is on the city's website. If the leak is past the meter or inside the house, call a plumber.

A plumber can often tell within a few minutes which side the leak is on by closing the house valve and watching the meter. If the meter keeps spinning with every valve in the house closed, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house, and the owner usually handles it.

Before we arrive

Before we arrive in Prineville: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak

Most Prineville emergencies start at a drain, a heater or a supply line, so these steps begin with the main valve.

1

Shut off the main valve

Find the main shutoff where the water line enters the house, often in a utility room, garage or crawlspace. Turn it until it stops, then open the lowest faucet to drain the pipes. If the valve will not move, do not force it, and tell dispatch.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off the breakers for the wet room, the laundry and the water heater before you step into the space. An electric tank should lose power before it drains. Never touch a cord or a switch while standing in water.

3

Clear the immediate area

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone and photograph the damage before you move anything. If the leak is at the water heater, close its cold supply valve. Keep children and pets away from standing water.

4

Call our 24/7 line

Call (503) 974-3329 and say where the water is coming from and whether the main is off. Mention whether the home is inside the city limits or out in the county. That tells dispatch whether the city or a private well supplies the house.
