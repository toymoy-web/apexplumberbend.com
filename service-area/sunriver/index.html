The vacant-house problem

You open the door and there is water on the floor

Sunriver has a large share of second homes and vacation rentals, and that changes how plumbing fails. A permanent household notices a drip within a day. A house that sits empty for two weeks can run a leak for fourteen days before anyone walks in.

An emergency plumber in Sunriver gets calls that start with a neighbor, a property manager or a returning owner. The leak is often small: a washing machine hose, a water heater fitting or a line that split during a cold night with the heat set low. The damage comes from the time it ran.

Sunriver's elevation, about 4,170 to 4,190 feet, puts it in a colder band than Bend. A weekend away with the thermostat turned down is enough to freeze an exposed line.

Before you leave

A Sunriver shutdown checklist

Before you leave for more than a few days

  • 1Close the main water valve and open a low faucet to relieve pressure.
  • 2Turn the water heater to vacation or off, and drain it if the house will be empty through winter.
  • 3Keep the heat at a safe minimum if the plan is to leave water on, and open cabinet doors on exterior walls.
  • 4Ask a neighbor or manager to check the house after a hard freeze.
  • 5Leave the shutoff location, plumber contact and insurance details where a caretaker can find them.
Who runs the utilities

Water and sewer in Sunriver are privately operated

The Sunriver Owners Association says on its website that it does not manage utility-type services. Water and sewer come from a private utility company in the community, and the association keeps a list of the providers. Sunriver itself has a sewer system, which is not true of every property around it.

On a plumbing emergency inside a house, the first move is the main valve and a call to the plumbing line. If water is leaking in the street or at the meter, the utility is the right call. A plumber can usually tell on arrival which side the problem is on.

Community rules also affect repairs. A planned community like Sunriver has design rules for exterior work, and a plumber should ask before changing anything visible from the street.

Hot tubs and big water heaters

Amenities that raise the plumbing stakes

Many Sunriver homes carry a hot tub, an extra bathroom or two, and a water heater sized for a full house of guests. The supply and drain lines serving those features run through unheated decks and crawlspaces, and they are the lines that fail when a house is left cold.

For the tub itself, ask the hot tub service about winterizing, since the plumbing side stops at the supply and drain connections. A shutoff for the tub's supply line that turns freely, and a drain that is clear, are both worth confirming before the first hard freeze.

Before we arrive

Before we arrive in Sunriver: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak

A house that sat empty needs a different first five minutes, so these steps assume nobody has been inside for days.

1

Shut off the main valve

Walk in with the main in mind. In many Sunriver homes it sits in a utility closet or at the crawlspace access near the water heater. Close it before you do anything else, then open a low faucet to relieve pressure. A house that sat empty may have been leaking for days.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off the breakers for any wet room, the water heater and the hot tub, which often has its own circuit. If the floor is flooded, do not wade to the panel. Wait outside and let dispatch advise on the safest next step.

3

Clear the immediate area

Photograph everything before you touch it, including the ceiling, the floor and the source if you can see it. Move guest belongings and electronics out of the wet zone. Tell the owner or manager and keep the guest dates for the insurance claim.

4

Call our 24/7 line

Call (503) 974-3329 and say whether the house is a rental, a second home or a full-time residence. Tell dispatch about gates, lockboxes and where to park. Mention a hot tub or a large water heater if you have one.

Common repairs in Sunriver

The calls that come from resort homes

Plumbing work in a resort community still follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), plus any community design rules. Resort homes often have large hot tubs, extra bathrooms and tankless or oversized water heaters. That makes water heater service and tankless heater service frequent requests. A kitchen full of guests brings the occasional disposal jam, and lines that sat unused need leak location after a long vacancy.
