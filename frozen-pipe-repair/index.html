Thawing safely

How to thaw a frozen pipe without causing a flood

Work from the faucet back toward the freeze

  • 1Shut off the main water valve before you start, in case the pipe has split where you cannot see.
  • 2Open the faucet closest to the frozen section so melting water and steam can escape.
  • 3Warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer on low or a heating pad, starting at the faucet end.
  • 4Never use an open flame, a propane torch or a space heater near the pipe.
  • 5If you cannot find the frozen section, or the pipe is bulging, stop and call a plumber.
Emergency

Frozen pipe emergency: act before the pipe splits

A frozen pipe is an emergency when no water comes out, when a pipe is bulging or when a wall or ceiling shows damp spots. Ice can crack a pipe without any sign, and the leak shows up when it thaws.

Close the main first, open the nearest faucet and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer on low. Never use an open flame. If you cannot reach the frozen section, our emergency plumber can thaw it safely and check for splits.

When to stop

Signs you should not keep trying yourself

A frozen pipe that you cannot see or reach is more dangerous than one in plain view. If the pipe is inside a wall, under a slab or behind finished ceilings, shut off the water. Then wait for a plumber who can find it with the right tools.

Other signs to stop are a bulging pipe, a split or crack, water stains forming near the pipe, or a faucet that suddenly runs when the pipe thaws. Each one means water is already escaping, and repair is the next step, as on the burst pipe repair page.

A plumber thaws pipes with controlled methods and then checks the line for cracks before water is turned back on, and any pipe replaced afterward follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Oregon. A pipe can freeze, thaw and leak without anyone noticing for days.

Stop the repeat

Permanent fixes after a freeze

Insulate and seal

Foam sleeves on supply lines slow heat loss, and sealing gaps around pipes where they pass through walls stops cold air from blowing across them. Closing foundation vents in winter is among the most effective single steps.

Heat cable on a pipe that cannot be insulated is another option, but it needs correct installation and a thermostat. Poorly installed heat cable can itself be a fire risk.

Reroute or relocate

If a pipe freezes every winter, the cause is where it runs. A plumber can reroute the line to a warmer path, move a valve inside the thermal envelope or convert a hose bib to a frost-free type.

Frost-free hose bibs still need the hose disconnected. A hose left on traps water in the barrel, where it freezes and splits the pipe behind the wall.

Wells and second homes

Special cases: wells, vacation homes and rentals

Well systems add a pressure tank, a switch and a short run of exposed pipe that can freeze in an enclosure. A small thermostat-controlled heater is the usual protection, and the private well repair page explains the failure signs.

Vacation homes in Sunriver and Sisters can sit empty through a cold week. A proper drain-down, with water shut off and lines blown out, is safer than keeping the heat low. Rental managers should include winterization on their seasonal checklist.
