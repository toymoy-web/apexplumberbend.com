Why it needs service

Your tankless heater is throwing codes or running cool

People call for tankless service for a few reasons. An error code shows on the display, hot water comes and goes, or the unit has gone years without a flush. Most of these trace back to scale, a restricted vent or intake, or a clogged inlet filter.

A tankless heater holds far less water than a tank, so small restrictions show up quickly. Soft city water in Bend slows scale. Private wells can build it much faster, and the heat exchanger is the first place it shows. The only way to know is to inspect the heat exchanger and flush the unit.

Tankless water heater service in Bend covers diagnosis, descaling, filter cleaning, vent and intake checks and a combustion test on gas units. If the unit is beyond repair, tank replacement lays out the options.

The flush

What a flush and descale actually involves

1

Isolate the unit

Service valves are closed and the heater is drained.

2

Circulate descaler

A pump runs a descaling solution through the heat exchanger.

3

Rinse and clean filters

The system is flushed and inlet screens are cleaned.

4

Test and verify

Flow, temperature and combustion are checked.

The job is simple if the unit has isolation valves. If it does not, the plumber can add them, which makes every future flush faster.

Conditions in Central Oregon

Winter, gas supply and siting

Cold incoming water

In winter, water entering the house is much colder than in summer, and the unit has to heat it further. A heater that handles two showers in July may struggle with them in January because the flow it can deliver falls as the temperature rise grows.

If your hot water fades only in winter, the unit may be undersized for cold weather, not faulty. A service call should include a flow and temperature test.

Gas lines and venting

Gas tankless units use a lot of fuel at once. A gas line that was fine for a tank may be too small, which shows up as ignition faults when other appliances run. Vents also need the right slope, clearance and condensate drain for condensing models.

These are code matters under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and an installer should confirm them at every service.

Safety first

Gas and carbon monoxide checks before any service

What to do if something seems wrong

  • 1If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not operate switches or phones inside.
  • 2Make sure working carbon monoxide alarms are installed near sleeping areas and the heater, and test them monthly.
  • 3Never block the vent or intake, and keep stored items and chemicals away from the unit.
  • 4If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside first and call for help from there.
  • 5Have gas connections and combustion checked by a licensed plumber, not tested with flame or guesswork.
Maintenance plan

Keeping a tankless heater healthy

A simple yearly routine

  • 1Flush the unit annually, or as the manufacturer states, more often on well water.
  • 2Clean the inlet water filter and the cold water screen.
  • 3Check the vent and intake for blockage, nests and ice.
  • 4Test the condensate drain on condensing models.
  • 5Record the date and the settings so the next visit can compare.
