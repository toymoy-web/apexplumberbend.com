No hot water

When the water heater quits in a Bend winter

A cold shower in January is the kind of failure people do not put off. Water heaters rarely die without warning, though. Rumbling from the tank, rust-colored hot water, a tripped breaker or a pilot that keeps going out are the usual early signs, and each one points to a different repair.

Water heater repair in Bend begins with a safety check. If water is pooling under the tank, the supply valve gets closed first. If you smell gas, everyone leaves the house and the gas utility or 911 is called from outside. Only after that does the diagnosis start.

Under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), a relief valve needs a discharge pipe that ends where escaping hot water cannot hurt anyone, and a plumber should confirm yours does.

Many faults are small. A bad heating element, a failed thermostat or a worn thermocouple can be replaced in a single visit on a tank that is otherwise healthy. A leaking tank cannot be repaired, and that is the point where water heater installation becomes the honest answer.

Emergency

Water heater emergency: leaks, no hot water and what to shut off

A leaking tank is the urgent case. Water pooling at the base can be a loose fitting or a failed tank, and a failed tank cannot be repaired. Close the cold water inlet and turn off power or gas to the heater right away.

No hot water in a Bend winter is also a real emergency for a household with children, older adults or a freeze risk. Our emergency line can usually tell you from your description whether the fix is a thermostat, an element, a pilot or a replacement.

Shut these off first

  • 1Close the cold water valve on top of the tank.
  • 2Turn off the breaker for an electric heater or the gas control for a gas heater.
  • 3Open a hot tap upstairs to relieve pressure only if water is not coming from the tank itself.
  • 4Keep people and pets away from standing water near electrical parts.

Repair or replace

How to decide whether the tank is worth fixing

A simple rule of thumb

If the heater is under about eight years old and the fault is a part, repair usually wins. Between eight and twelve years, repair is reasonable only for a cheap part. Past twelve, a repair that costs a large share of a new unit rarely pays back.

A tank that leaks from the body is finished regardless of age. Water coming from the relief valve or a fitting is different and often fixable, so do not assume the worst until a plumber has looked.

What a repair visit includes

The plumber checks the power or gas supply, tests the elements or burner, measures the thermostat setting, inspects the relief valve and drain valve, and looks at the anode rod. You get the findings and a price before parts are ordered.

The anode rod is worth asking about. It is a sacrificial metal rod that slows tank corrosion, and replacing a depleted one can add years to a tank that is otherwise sound.

Local conditions

What Central Oregon changes about water heaters

Bend city water is on the soft side, around one to one and a half grains of hardness per gallon, so heavy scale is less of a reason for failure here than in harder-water towns. Sediment still collects, and an annual flush keeps a tank quieter and the elements cleaner. Homes on private wells can differ, and a water test shows what the heater is dealing with.

Cold garages and crawlspaces matter more than water chemistry. A tank in an unheated space loses heat faster in winter, and exposed supply lines near it are the first pipes to freeze. If your heater sits outside the heated envelope, insulating the lines and checking the space for drafts is cheap protection.

Gas heaters need a clear flue and combustion air, and installers follow the manufacturer's rules for elevation, since Bend sits about 3,600 feet above sea level. A repair visit should include a look at the venting, not only the burner.

By fuel type

Gas, electric and propane tanks fail differently

Electric tanks usually have two elements and two thermostats. If the lower element fails you get less hot water, and if the upper one fails you get lukewarm water or none. A breaker that trips repeatedly points to a shorted element, not a bad breaker, and it should not be reset over and over.

Gas tanks depend on a flame, a thermocouple or flame sensor, and a gas control valve. A pilot that will not stay lit is often a worn thermocouple, which is a modest repair. A scorched or sooted burner, or any smell of gas, is a reason to stop and call.

Some homes outside town, including many near Sunriver and Sisters, use propane tanks. Propane heaters work like natural gas units for repair purposes, but the supply side differs, and running out of fuel looks exactly like a dead pilot. Check the tank gauge before calling.

Prevent the next call

Maintenance that actually extends tank life

Once a year, in this order

  • 1Turn the thermostat down, shut the supply and flush a few gallons from the drain valve to clear sediment.
  • 2Test the temperature and pressure relief valve by lifting the lever briefly, if the valve is accessible and in good condition.
  • 3Inspect the anode rod every few years and replace it when it is more than half consumed.
  • 4Check for corrosion on fittings and the area around the base for slow leaks.
  • 5Set the thermostat near 120 degrees Fahrenheit, which reduces scald risk and slows scale and standby loss.
Do this first

Safe steps when the hot water stops

Before you call

  • 1Look under and around the tank. If water is leaking, close the cold-water supply valve on top of the heater.
  • 2On an electric tank, check the breaker. On a gas tank, look at the pilot window, but do not relight it if you smell gas.
  • 3Turn the thermostat to a normal setting if someone lowered it, then wait an hour before assuming the unit is dead.
  • 4Never plug or cap the relief valve discharge pipe, even if it drips.
  • 5Photograph the data label showing model, serial number and manufacture date for the plumber.

Leaking tank

A leaking water heater: repair, flush or replace

Water at the base of a tank has three common sources. A loose fitting or a relief valve can be repaired. A failed drain valve can be replaced. A leak from the shell means the tank has corroded through and the unit must be replaced, which is more likely as a tank ages.

No hot water is a separate call and often a cheap fix. A tripped breaker, a failed element, a gas control or a pilot light will do it. Dispatch asks a few questions before the visit so the truck carries the right part.
