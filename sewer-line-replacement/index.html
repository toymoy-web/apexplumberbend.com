Emergency

Emergency sewer line replacement: when the line has failed

Replacement becomes urgent when a camera shows a collapsed pipe, a long crack, a bellied section or roots that return right after cleaning. The house cannot use its drains safely until the line works again.

Our emergency crew stabilizes the situation first, then explains the options, which can include open trench, pipe bursting or lining. You get the price before work starts, and any permit is handled as the work requires.

Ways to replace a lateral

Open trench, pipe bursting and lining

Open trench

The plumber digs along the line, removes the old pipe and lays new pipe at the correct slope. It is the only method that fixes a sag or a collapsed section directly and lets the crew see every joint.

The trade-off is the dig. Landscaping, driveways and walks may need to be restored, and the quote should say how much restoration is included.

Pipe bursting and lining

Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old one, breaking the old pipe outward. It limits digging to access pits at each end. Lining coats the existing pipe with resin and suits pipes that are intact but leaking.

Neither works well on a pipe with a severe sag or a major collapse. A plumber should explain why one method fits and the others do not.

Rules and responsibility

Permits, utility locates and who pays

Replacing a sewer lateral is regulated work under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so it normally requires a permit and an inspection. The licensed contractor should pull the permit and schedule the inspection, and your quote should say so.

Oregon utilities offer a free locate service before any digging. Calling 811 marks buried gas, electric and water lines so the crew does not strike them. A contractor who skips this step is putting your property at risk.

The City of Bend's standards put the sewer service in the owner's hands from the main into the property, which includes any private pump on a pressure sewer system. Work that reaches the street or right of way still has its own rules and may need the city's inspector, so ask about both before a crew digs.

Some Bend properties that recently moved from septic to sewer have a pressure system with a private grinder pump, and the owner maintains that pump. Tell the plumber if you have one, because a pump failure looks like a clog but is a different repair.

Warning signs

Signs a lateral is near the end

Common reasons homeowners call

  • 1Backups in more than one fixture, especially the lowest drain in the house.
  • 2Gurgling toilets after laundry or showers.
  • 3A sewer smell near the foundation or in the yard.
  • 4Soggy, unusually green patches of lawn above the line.
  • 5A camera showing roots, offset joints or flaking pipe walls.
  • 6A cleanout that has been snaked many times in a single year.
