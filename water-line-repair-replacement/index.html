Which pipe is this?

The water line is the pipe between the street and your house

Your water line, also called the service line, carries water from the meter or, on a private well, from the well and pressure tank to the house. It is a separate pipe from the plumbing inside the walls. When it leaks or fails, the whole house is affected at once, and the leak is often buried where you cannot see it.

Water line repair and replacement in Bend covers that buried run. If the problem is inside the house, repiping is the page you want. If you only suspect a leak somewhere and want it found first, leak location comes before any digging.

Emergency

Water line emergency: broken service line or yard leak

A broken service line sends water into the ground at full pressure. Signs include a soggy patch in the yard, a sudden drop in pressure, a meter that keeps spinning or no water at all at the house.

Close the main valve at the house and call. Our emergency plumber locates the break, explains whether a spot repair or a replacement section is the better fix and gives the price before digging starts.

Fix choices

Repair a section or replace the whole line

Spot repair

If a camera or locator shows one failed fitting or one split section, the plumber digs a small trench at that point, cuts out the bad pipe and joins new pipe with approved fittings. It limits the dig and the cost, and suits a line that is otherwise sound.

A repair makes sense when the pipe is modern and the leak is isolated. A line that has leaked in several places usually means the pipe itself is aging.

Full replacement

Replacement lays a new line along the old route or a better one. Trenchless methods can pull new pipe through the old path with small access pits at each end. Open trench gives a straight-line view of the whole run and allows rerouting around trees or structures.

Common modern materials include copper and polyethylene pipe. Which one fits depends on soil, depth, length and code. Ask the plumber to explain the choice rather than naming the cheapest.

Who owns which section

Meter, curb stop and the shared boundary

On a utility-served property, the water provider typically owns the pipe up to and including the meter, and the property owner owns the line from the meter to the house. That boundary is where a leak becomes your repair. The provider in Bend may be the city or Avion Water Company depending on your street, so confirm with whichever sends your bill.

If the leak is before the meter, call the provider. If it is after, the plumber handles it, and the work is regulated under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A permit and inspection are normal for a replacement.

On a private well, the entire line from the well to the house is yours. That includes the pitless adapter at the wellhead, the buried pipe and any wiring that shares the trench. Say so on the call, because it changes who needs to be involved.

Which side leaks

Meter side or house side? Well to house?

Callers often know a leak exists but not where. If the line is dead after everything is closed, the break is between the meter and the house, and the line from a private well to the house follows the same logic. A plumber isolates sections and tests them until the leak is found.

Owners also ask for a quote to replace the entire run. Expect a plumber to confirm the route, depth and surface restoration before pricing, and to explain the permit steps the work needs.
